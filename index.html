How a player is graded

1. Base points. Per-game stats are converted with your scoring:

2. This week. The model adjusts base points, then blends in the sites:

Model = Base × Matchup × Trend × Vegas × Weather × Health Matchup = 1 + weight × (oppRank − 16.5) / 15.5 Vegas = 1 + weight × (team implied pts − team's usual pts) / team's usual pts Weather = wind 15+ mph: passing −5%, kickers −10% (20+ mph: −10% / −18%) Health = injury status (live from ESPN every 3 minutes), adjusted by Wed–Fri practice (DNP / limited / full) Projection = (1 − w) × Model + w × average(ESPN, Sleeper, CBS, FantasyPros) w is tuned by position from past accuracy (see Accuracy), unless set in Settings On bye, Out, or IR → 0

Base is season points per game. Matchup rank is how many fantasy points the opponent has allowed to that position per game (this season; 32 = most allowed). Trend compares snap share over the last two games to the season (for QBs, recent points). Health is the current injury designation. Schedule averages the matchup ranks of the remaining opponents. Start / Flex / Sit calls assume a 12-team league.

Start/sit tools. Each player is also run through up to seven tools: this model, the FantasyPros expert consensus, ESPN's analysts, CBS's three experts, and the ESPN, Sleeper and CBS projections. Each tool's rank at the position becomes a vote (12-team league: QB/TE top 12 start, RB top 24, WR top 30; the next tier is flex). The Start / Sit call uses the average rank across tools.

3. Floor and ceiling. Half from the position's typical swing (QB ±22%, RB ±30%, WR ±35%, TE ±38%, K ±35%, DST ±45%) and half from the player's own week-to-week swings once he has 3+ games. Two players with close projections? The narrower range is the safer start.

4. Rest of season.

Pts/game = Base × Schedule × Trend × Health Games left = 18 − week (minus bye if still ahead) Total = Pts/game × Games left

Kickers and defenses use fantasy points per game under your kicker and D/ST rules.

Data sources

Stats that predict fantasy points

Research on fantasy scoring keeps finding the same thing: opportunity predicts points better than efficiency. Volume stats are stable from week to week, while yards per carry and TD rates swing a lot in small samples. Player pages rank every stat below against the rest of the position.

Usage

  • Snap share – playing time, the base of everything else.
  • Target share and WOPR – the strongest predictors for WRs and TEs.
  • Air yards share and average depth of target – downfield role.
  • Carry share and opportunities per game for RBs.
  • Red-zone, end-zone and goal-line shares – touchdown opportunity, steadier than TD totals.
  • First-read targets – how often the QB looks to him first.
  • Expected fantasy points – what a player's usage is worth, and whether he's scoring above or below it.

Efficiency and context

  • Yards per team pass attempt, yards per snap and EPA – production per opportunity.
  • Separation, YAC over expected and catchable target rate for receivers.
  • Rush yards over expected, stacked boxes, yards after contact and broken tackles for RBs.
  • Completion % over expected, time to throw and interception-worthy throws for QBs.
  • Vegas implied totals, weather, practice reports and expert consensus – used in each week's projections.
